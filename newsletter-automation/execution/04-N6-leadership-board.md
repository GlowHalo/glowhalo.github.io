## 컨셉

**이름**: The Leadership Board
**한 줄 태그라인**: A weekly second opinion for people who manage other people — three

**핵심 아이디어**: N1~N3와 동일한 메커니즘(서로 사전에 답을 안 보는 독립된 AI 3인이 한 딜레마를

**N1·N2·N3와의 차별화**:

- N2(The Career Board) — "내가 이 결정을 내려도 될까" 개인 커리어·자기이해 딜레마.
- N3(The Builder's Board) — "이 기능을 만들어야 할까" AI 툴 빌더의 제품 판단.
- **N6(The Leadership Board)** — "이 사람을, 이 팀을, 이 조직을 어떻게 이끌어야 할까" **사람을
  관리하는 입장**의 딜레마. 대상은 창업가에 국한되지 않고 팀장~C레벨 등 **기존 기업 안의
  관리자·임원**까지 넓힘 — N1보다 청중이 넓고, 자기 사업이 없어도 구독할 이유가 있다.

## 창간호 초안

**제목**: Welcome to The Leadership Board — Why "Everyone Likes Them" Isn't a Reason to Keep Them

---

Every leader eventually hits the same test: is this the right call, or just the comfortable one?
This newsletter runs the experiment differently — every week, one real leadership dilemma gets
torn apart by three independent AI perspectives who never see each other's answers first.

**This week's board**

Dilemma: *"A team member consistently misses their targets, but the whole team loves working with
them. Do I let them go?"* (one of the hardest calls any manager faces, chosen because it's
instructive, not because it has one right answer)

- **Team verdict — HANDLE THE PROCESS, NOT JUST THE OUTCOME.** How you exit someone matters more
  to team trust than that you did. A well-liked underperformer let go abruptly reads as "results
  are all that matter here" — the same person let go after a documented, fair chance to improve
  reads as "this team holds a real bar." The team is watching the process, not just the headcount.
- **Execution verdict — QUANTIFY THE DRAG BEFORE YOU DECIDE.** "Consistently misses targets" is
  doing a lot of work in that sentence — is it 10% off or 50% off? Is the gap shrinking or
  growing? A vague sense of underperformance isn't a decision input; a number and a trend are. If
  the drag is real and compounding, waiting for likability to catch up to output rarely works.
- **Precedent verdict — THIS IS THE POLICY, NOT JUST THIS PERSON.** Whatever you decide here
  becomes the unwritten rule for every future underperformer who happens to be well-liked. Decide
  the policy first ("we act on sustained target misses after a documented improvement plan") and
  then apply it — don't let one relationship-heavy case set a precedent you didn't actually
  choose.

**Composite: Give a real, time-boxed improvement plan first — not as a formality, but because it
answers the Execution question (does the gap close?) and the Team question (did we give a fair
shot?) at the same time. If it doesn't close, the Precedent question answers itself.**

**Board prompt of the week**

"Separate the Decision from the Relationship" — full prompt is one of the 15 in the
[AI Board of Directors](https://glowhalo.gumroad.com/l/ai-board-of-directors) pack, for readers
who want a standing board of AI advisors for calls like this one, not just this week's.
